Why probate and inherited property leads are different

A typical borrower is shopping. An heir is often grieving, handling paperwork they have never seen before, and waiting on a process they do not control. They may be an executor, a sibling, a spouse or an adult child, and they do not always agree with each other.

For a loan officer that changes the job. The goal is not a fast pitch. It is to be easy to find, calm to talk to, and useful before anyone is ready to apply. Our 2026 playbook, the probate boom guide for MLOs, goes deeper on timing and prospecting.

The types of inherited property leads you will meet

  • The heir who wants to keep the house. They are searching for an inherited property mortgage or a way to refinance inherited property once the estate allows it.
  • The sibling buyout. One family member wants to keep the home and needs to buy out the others, a search often phrased as "buy out siblings inherited house."
  • The heir who sells. The property goes on the market, which creates purchase-side work and a natural link to local real estate agents.
  • The estate sale buyer. Probate property financing questions also come from buyers purchasing a home out of an estate.

Each one needs a different first conversation, which is one more reason local pages that answer these questions plainly are worth building.

Follow-up that fits a sensitive moment

Speed matters, but tone matters more. An automated message that sounds like a pitch lands badly with a grieving family. AI lead follow-up can acknowledge an inquiry quickly and set a calm expectation, which gets you through the first hour. After that, the human conversation is yours.

Plan your follow-up around these principles:

  • Acknowledge the situation before mentioning any product.
  • Ask what the family is trying to accomplish and on what timeline.
  • Stay out of legal advice. Questions about wills, trusts, title or the court process belong with an estate attorney.
  • Offer to wait. A family that is not ready this month may be ready next quarter.
