The hardest part of posting consistently is the blank screen. An AI social media marketing writer removes it: you describe your business and your market, and it drafts the posts, hooks and calls to action for you to edit. The YPN USA writer is built for mortgage loan officers and real estate professionals, it is free to try, and you do not need an account to run it.

Generate free posts

Everything it returns is labeled draft marketing copy. It writes text for you to review. It does not log in to Facebook, Instagram or LinkedIn, and it does not schedule or publish anything.

What you get from one run

Each run returns plain text under seven headings:

  • Content angle. The idea that ties the batch together.
  • Five post drafts. Each one distinct and ready to edit.
  • Five hooks. Opening lines to test on their own.
  • CTA options. Different ways to end a post.
  • Hashtag and topic ideas. Starting points for reach.
  • Compliance check. Reminders to review before publishing.
  • 30-day follow-up ideas. Where to take the topic next.

The follow-up section gives ideas, not a finished calendar. Think of the result as a week of material and a month of directions.

What you enter

The form asks for your profession (mortgage or real estate), business name, market and services. You can also add an audience, such as first-time buyers or referral partners, a platform, and a topic or offer. If you leave the platform blank, it writes for Instagram, Facebook and LinkedIn. If you leave the topic blank, it defaults to educational local-market content.

A tighter brief gives a better draft. "Home buyers in Visalia who are unsure where to start" beats "people who want a house."

A simple weekly routine

The writer works best as a habit, not a one-off. Pick one topic a week and run it. Keep the two or three drafts that sound most like you, and edit them for your voice and your facts. Read each post against your compliance requirements, publish, and note which hooks earn replies. Next week, feed that learning back into the topic box.

Facts only, by design

The writer is instructed to draft from the facts you supply and not to invent rates, listings, closings, testimonials, credentials, market statistics, guarantees or regulatory claims. If you want a post about a specific listing or a specific event, put the details in the topic box yourself. A mortgage content generator that makes up numbers is a liability, so this one is told not to.

For loan officers

A mortgage social media writer has to be careful. The tool is instructed to avoid promises of approval, savings, rates or outcomes, and it prompts a compliance review before you publish. Treat that as a prompt, not a sign-off. Your advertising, licensing and disclosure obligations stay with you, and every post should be checked against them before it goes out.

Good topics for a loan officer social media writer include how the process works, what to gather before a first conversation, and how you work with local agents. Pairing posts with a Realtor partner is the idea behind realtor co-marketing.

For realtors and real estate teams

A realtor social media writer is most useful for the content that is easy to put off: neighborhood guides, buyer and seller education, and local-market commentary. Because the tool will not invent listings or sales, supply your real details when you want a post about them. Teams can run it with a different audience or platform for each agent and compare the drafts. Your Google profile is another place to use these ideas, so pair it with the Google Business Profile Optimizer.

Give every post somewhere to send people

A post with no destination is only a vanity metric. For loan officers, YPN USA gives posts a place to land: hyper-local SEO pages, a branded MLO website, and AI follow-up when someone responds. An exclusive ZIP territory means the attention you build locally comes back to you. Check or claim a ZIP or compare plans on the pricing page.
