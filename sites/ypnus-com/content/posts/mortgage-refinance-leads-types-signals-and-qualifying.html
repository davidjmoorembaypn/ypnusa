Most mortgage refinance leads are a tax on your pipeline. Borrowers browse rates at 2 a.m. and submit forms to five aggregator sites. Three LOs call within minutes, and the borrower decides this is all too much and ghosts all of you. The refi lead you actually fund is a different animal: it arrives with a specific financial trigger, a clear equity position, and enough motivation to return your call.

This guide breaks down the three refinance lead subtypes, the quality signals that separate window-shoppers from borrowers ready to lock, and the discount-point math experienced LOs use to qualify them on the first call. If you are building or rebuilding a refi pipeline, this is the operating manual.

The Rate Environment Refinance Leads Live In

Refinance leads are rate-sensitive by definition, but not in the way most LOs think. A 50-basis-point drop does not automatically create a refi boom. What creates refi opportunity is the spread between a borrower's current note rate and today's market rate, adjusted for closing costs, mortgage insurance changes, and the borrower's remaining amortization schedule. A borrower who got a 7.125% rate six months ago may benefit from a refi at 6.625%. A borrower at 6.5% probably does not.

The math that matters is the breakeven: how many months of payment savings does it take to recover the closing costs? If a borrower saves $200 per month and closing costs run $4,200, the breakeven is 21 months. If the borrower plans to move in 18 months, the refi does not make sense even though the rate is lower. This is why generic rate-drop alerts generate window-shoppers. The borrower sees a lower number on a screen but has not run the breakeven, and when you finally get them on the phone, the deal falls apart on cost recovery.

For loan officers sourcing refi leads through mortgage lead channels, the rate environment determines which subtype of refi lead is most abundant. When rates drop meaningfully, rate-and-term refis dominate. When rates are flat or rising, cash-out refis and streamline refinances become the primary opportunities because borrowers are pulling equity or reducing paperwork, not chasing a lower rate.

Three Refinance Lead Types and Their Quality Signals

Not all refi leads are the same product. Rate-and-term, cash-out, and streamline refinances each have different borrower motivations, qualification hurdles, and quality signals that tell you whether the lead is fundable. Treating them as one bucket is why LOs waste hours working leads that were never going to close.

Rate-and-Term Refinance Leads

These borrowers want a lower monthly payment, a shorter term, or both. They are not pulling cash out. Their motivation is purely financial optimization, which means the deal lives or dies on the numbers.

Quality signals that matter:

  • Current rate vs. market rate spread: At least 50 basis points is the typical threshold for a meaningful rate-and-term refi. Below that, closing costs eat the savings too fast.
  • Months since origination: Borrowers who closed within the last 12 to 18 months are the sweet spot. Their loan balance is close to the original principal, and their rate is likely above current market if rates have moved.
  • Remaining loan term: A borrower with 25 years left on a 30-year mortgage has room to refinance into a lower rate without resetting amortization too aggressively. A borrower with 8 years left rarely benefits unless they are intentionally extending.
  • Mortgage insurance status: If the borrower is currently paying MI and home values have risen, a refi may drop the MI entirely. That savings can justify a refi even with a modest rate improvement. This is one of the most overlooked quality signals.

The window-shopper version of this lead has a rate spread of 25 basis points or less, has already been shopping with three lenders, and wants you to quote a rate without pulling credit. The serious borrower knows their current rate, knows their approximate balance, and asks about breakeven. That question alone tells you they have done the math.

Cash-Out Refinance Leads

Cash-out borrowers are not rate-shopping. They need liquidity: home improvements, debt consolidation, tuition, or business capital. Their motivation is need-based, which makes them less rate-sensitive and more likely to close if the numbers work. But the qualification hurdles are higher because the lender is increasing the loan amount and the LTV.

Quality signals that matter:

  • Equity position: The borrower needs at least 20% equity post-cash-out for conventional loans to avoid pricing adjustments and MI triggers. A borrower at 75% LTV wanting to pull cash to 80% is workable. A borrower already at 85% LTV is a dead lead for conventional cash-out.
  • Stated use of funds: Debt consolidation borrowers tend to close at higher rates because the monthly savings are obvious even with a rate increase. Home improvement borrowers close steadily. "Investment" or "business opportunity" borrowers are the least reliable because the use of funds is speculative.
  • DTI trajectory: A borrower consolidating $30,000 in credit card debt at 22% APR into a mortgage at 6.5% sees their overall DTI drop dramatically. The LO who can frame this math on the first call wins the deal. A borrower whose DTI is already at 50% with no consolidation benefit is not a cash-out candidate.
  • Credit score stability: Cash-out refinances often carry LLPA adjustments for lower credit scores. A borrower at 740+ faces minimal adjustments. A borrower at 660 may see pricing that erases the benefit of the cash-out, especially at higher LTVs.

Streamline Refinance Leads

Streamline refinances (FHA Streamline, VA IRRRL, and certain conventional limited cash-out refis) are the lowest-friction refi product. No appraisal, reduced documentation, and faster closing timelines. They are also the most overlooked lead source because LOs assume borrowers with these loans will refinance on their own. They will not. They need you to tell them the opportunity exists.

Quality signals that matter:

  • Current loan type: FHA and VA loans originated in the 2022 to 2024 rate cycle are prime streamline candidates. The existing MI on FHA loans carries forward, which changes the breakeven math but does not eliminate the benefit if the rate drop is sufficient.
  • Payment history: Streamline programs require timely mortgage payments, typically no 30-day lates in the most recent 12 months. A borrower with a clean payment history is pre-qualified for the program requirement before you even pull credit.
  • Net tangible benefit: Most streamline programs require a measurable benefit to the borrower, usually defined as a reduction in principal and interest payment, a lower rate, or a shorter term. The exact threshold varies by program and lender overlay.
  • Seasoning period: FHA Streamlines generally require the loan to be seasoned at least 210 days with at least six payments made. VA IRRRLs have similar seasoning requirements. A borrower three months into their loan is not eligible yet.

Streamline leads convert fast when you reach the right borrower at the right time. The contact rate is higher because the borrower is not comparison-shopping across five lenders. They have an FHA or VA loan, they know refinancing is possible, and they want a simple process. The LO who calls with a specific payment reduction number on the first contact usually wins.

Sourcing Refi Leads: In-House vs. Vendor vs. Trigger Data

Where you get refinance leads determines their quality more than any script or follow-up system can fix. Three sourcing models exist, and each has a different cost structure, exclusivity profile, and compliance footprint.

In-House Database Mining

Your closed loans from the past three years are the cheapest refi leads you will ever work. You already know their rate, their loan type, their payment history, and their contact information. The acquisition cost was paid years ago. The only cost now is the labor to segment and outreach.

Segment your book by current rate, loan type, and origination date. Borrowers who closed at rates above today's market are your rate-and-term candidates. Borrowers with FHA loans in the seasoning window are your streamline candidates. Borrowers who have been in their homes for five-plus years and have seen appreciation are your cash-out candidates. This is not generic "find people with equity" advice. It is a targeted segmentation of your own book by refi subtype.

Vendor-Sourced Refi Leads

Vendor refi leads come from credit bureau triggers, web forms, and aggregator platforms. The quality ranges from genuinely exclusive to fully shared, and the price reflects the competition level. A true exclusive refi lead at $150 that closes at 4% produces a cost per funded loan around $3,750. A shared refi lead at $25 that closes at 1.5% produces a cost per funded loan above $2,000, and that assumes you are the first to call.

The problem with most vendor refi leads is that they are triggered by credit inquiries, which means the borrower is already being called by every LO who bought the trigger. If you are buying credit trigger refi leads, ask the vendor one question: how many other LOs receive the same trigger? If the answer is more than one, you are buying a shared lead with a premium price tag.

Life-Stage Trigger Data

Life-Stage Triggering is a different model. Instead of reacting to a credit pull, it surfaces refinance opportunities from public record events and life transitions that change a borrower's financing needs: divorce filings that require a buyout, probate cases where an heir needs to refinance to keep a property, marriage records that precede joint mortgage applications, and birthdays that mark the transition into retirement planning where reverse mortgage leads become relevant.

The advantage is timing and exclusivity. Life-stage trigger leads arrive before the borrower enters the aggregator ecosystem, which means you are the first LO to contact them, not the fourth. A borrower who has been in their home for seven years, has a clean payment history, and just filed for divorce may need a cash-out refi to buy out a spouse's share. That is a life-stage trigger that produces a specific refi subtype with a clear use of funds.

Qualifying Refinance Leads: DTI, Equity, and Discount Points

The first call with a refi lead is not a rate quote. It is a qualification conversation that determines whether the deal is fundable. Three numbers tell you most of what you need to know: debt-to-income ratio, equity position, and the discount-point math that determines whether buying down the rate actually benefits the borrower.

DTI: The First Gate

DTI is the first gate because it kills deals that look good on paper. A borrower with 30% equity and a 760 credit score cannot refinance if their DTI is 58%. For conventional refinances, the typical DTI ceiling is 50% with strong compensating factors, and many lenders cap at 45% for cash-out. For FHA Streamlines, the DTI calculation is simplified because income documentation is reduced, but the borrower still needs to demonstrate the ability to pay.

On the first call, ask for gross monthly income and total monthly debts including the current mortgage payment. If the borrower cannot answer both questions, they are not ready to move forward. If the DTI is above 50%, the conversation shifts to what would need to change: paying down a specific debt, waiting for a raise, or restructuring the loan to include a payoff that reduces monthly obligations.

Equity: The Second Gate

Equity determines product eligibility and pricing. For rate-and-term refis, most conventional lenders allow up to 95% LTV. For cash-out refis, the ceiling is typically 80% LTV for conventional and varies by program for government loans. But LTV is not the only number. The combined loan-to-value (CLTV) matters if the borrower has a second lien or HELOC that will remain in place.

Ask the borrower for a rough home value estimate and their current principal balance. If they do not know either number, check your servicing data or use an AVM. A borrower who thinks they have 40% equity but actually has 15% is a lead that will not survive underwriting. Better to find that out on the first call than after pulling credit and ordering an appraisal.

Discount Points: The Math Most LOs Skip

Discount points are where refi leads get won or lost, and most LOs handle them badly. The borrower asks whether they should buy down the rate, and the LO gives a vague answer about whether it "makes sense depending on how long you plan to stay in the home." That is not a qualification. That is a deflection.

Here is the actual math. One discount point costs 1% of the loan amount and typically reduces the rate by 0.25% (though the actual buydown varies by lender and market conditions). The decision comes down to breakeven: how many months of payment savings does it take to recover the cost of the point?

The formula: Discount Point Cost ÷ Monthly Payment Savings = Breakeven in Months.

Example: A borrower refinancing a $400,000 loan is offered a rate of 6.5% with no points or 6.25% with one point. The point costs $4,000. The payment at 6.5% is approximately $2,528. The payment at 6.25% is approximately $2,462. The monthly savings is $66. The breakeven is $4,000 ÷ $66 = 60.6 months, or roughly five years. If the borrower plans to stay in the home for more than five years, the point pays for itself. If they plan to move in three years, it does not.

This math matters for lead qualification because it separates borrowers who are serious about long-term savings from those who are chasing the lowest possible rate on paper. A borrower who asks about discount points and wants to see the breakeven calculation is a high-quality lead. A borrower who demands the lowest rate with zero points and zero closing costs is a rate-shopper who will leave you for a quarter-point the week before closing.

The LO who can run this math on the first call, in plain English, builds trust that no aggregator lead vendor can replicate. That trust is what converts a refi lead into a funded loan.

Reactivating Your Book for Refi Opportunities

Your closed-loan database is a refi pipeline that you already paid to build. Most LOs let it sit dormant because they do not have a system to segment and re-engage borrowers when rate conditions change. Life-Stage Triggering changes that equation by continuously scanning your book for borrowers whose current financial situation aligns with a refinance opportunity.

Here is how to run a book reactivation campaign for refi leads:

  • Step 1: Segment by current rate. Pull every closed loan from the past 36 months. Sort by note rate. Any borrower whose rate is 50+ basis points above today's market is a rate-and-term refi candidate.
  • Step 2: Segment by loan type. Flag every FHA and VA loan. Cross-reference against seasoning requirements (210 days for FHA Streamline, similar for VA IRRRL). Borrowers who meet seasoning and have clean payment histories are streamline candidates.
  • Step 3: Segment by equity age. Borrowers who closed five-plus years ago in markets that have appreciated are cash-out candidates. Use AVM data or county assessment records to estimate current LTV.
  • Step 4: Layer life-stage triggers. Cross-reference your book against public record events: divorce filings, probate cases, marriage records, and age milestones. A borrower from your book who appears in a divorce filing is a cash-out refi lead with a specific use of funds (buyout). That is not a cold lead. That is a warm contact with a concrete need.
  • Step 5: Launch segmented nurture campaigns. Each segment gets a different message. Rate-and-term borrowers get a payment comparison. Streamline borrowers get a simplified process pitch. Cash-out borrowers get a scenario analysis based on their estimated equity. All messaging should include RESPA-compliant language and TCPA-compliant opt-out mechanisms.

The borrowers who were not financeable six months ago may be viable now. Credit scores improve, debt gets paid down, and equity builds with appreciation. A database that looked dead in January may produce three funded loans by June if you have the system to re-engage it.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the biggest quality signal that a refinance lead is ready to lock?

A borrower asking about the breakeven period on discount points is a high-quality refi lead. They have done the math and want to understand whether buying down the rate pays off. A borrower demanding the lowest rate with zero points and zero closing costs is a rate-shopper who will jump for a quarter-point before closing.

How does Life-Stage Triggering surface refinance opportunities from my existing book?

Divorce filings can trigger a need to refinance a jointly held property to buy out one spouse's share. Probate cases can require an heir to refinance to keep an inherited property. These life-stage triggers surface specific refi subtypes (usually cash-out) with a concrete use of funds before the borrower enters the aggregator lead market.

What is the discount-point breakeven formula for qualifying refi leads?

Discount point cost divided by monthly payment savings equals breakeven in months. For example, a $4,000 point that saves $66 per month has a breakeven of about 61 months. If the borrower plans to stay beyond that period, the buydown is worth it.

What DTI and equity thresholds should I use to qualify refi leads on the first call?

Rate-and-term leads need a 50+ basis point spread and a breakeven under 36 months. Cash-out leads need 20% or more post-cash-out equity and a DTI that improves after the transaction. Streamline leads need an FHA or VA loan, clean payment history, and a net tangible benefit as defined by the program.

Are streamline refinance leads worth pursuing if rates have not dropped significantly?

No. Streamline refinances (FHA Streamline, VA IRRRL) are the lowest-friction refi product because they require no appraisal and reduced documentation. They are often overlooked because LOs assume borrowers will refinance on their own, but most will not without being contacted directly.
