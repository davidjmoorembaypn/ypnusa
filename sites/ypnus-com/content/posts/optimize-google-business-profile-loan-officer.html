Your Google Business Profile (GBP) is the highest-intent, lowest-cost lead source most loan officers ignore. When someone searches "mortgage lender near me," Google's local map pack appears above almost everything else. Here is the checklist to claim that spot.

1. Claim and verify your profile

Search your name + business on Google, claim the profile, and complete verification. An unverified profile cannot rank in the map pack.

2. Nail the core fields

Exact business name, your real service area (the cities you lend in), phone, and a link to your site. Consistency with how you appear elsewhere (your NMLS listing, your site) matters.

3. Choose the right categories

Primary: "Mortgage Lender" or "Mortgage Broker." Add relevant secondary categories. Categories are one of the strongest local ranking signals.

4. Write a keyword-aware description

Naturally mention the loan types and cities you serve (FHA, VA, refinance, first-time buyers in [your city]). Helpful, not stuffed.

5. List your services

Add each loan type as a service with a short description. This is free real estate that helps you match more searches.

6. Add photos that build trust

A real headshot, your office or city, and a few branded images. Profiles with photos get far more clicks and calls.

7. Post weekly

GBP posts (rate updates, tips, wins) keep your profile active and signal to Google you are current. See our 20 post ideas.

8. Earn reviews consistently

Reviews are a top-three local ranking factor. Have a simple, repeatable ask after every closing — here is how to get more reviews.

9. Keep NAP consistent everywhere

Your Name, Address (or service area), and Phone should match across your site, GBP, and directories. Inconsistencies confuse Google and cost rankings.

10. Respond to everything

Reply to every review and question. Engagement signals an active, trustworthy business.

Work this checklist and your profile becomes a steady, free source of local borrower leads — the kind that convert because they are searching with intent.

Category selection: the setting most loan officers get wrong

Your primary GBP category carries more ranking weight than almost anything else on the profile, and Google's category list for lending is genuinely confusing. Here is how the main options actually behave:

  • "Mortgage Lender" — the closest match for most individual loan officers originating loans directly. Use this as your primary category if you personally take applications and lock rates.
  • "Mortgage Broker" — correct if you're shopping a loan across multiple wholesale lenders rather than originating in-house. Do not use this if you work for a single lender or bank.
  • "Loan Agency" / "Financial Consultant" — too generic on their own; fine as a secondary category, weak as a primary because they don't signal mortgage intent to Google's local algorithm.

Pick one primary category that matches your actual licensing and business model, then add two or three accurate secondary categories (e.g., "Mortgage Broker" as secondary if you occasionally broker, or "Real Estate Consultant" if you do heavy Realtor co-marketing). Do not stack unrelated categories to "cast a wider net" — GBP's spam detection increasingly suppresses profiles that mismatch category and actual service, which can suppress your map-pack visibility entirely.

Where your NMLS number belongs (and where it does not)

Your NMLS number should appear in the GBP business description and, if the field is available in your region, in the "opening date" or attributes area is not the place — keep it out of the business name field entirely. Google frequently suspends profiles that stuff licensing numbers, credentials, or keywords into the name field because it reads as manipulation, not identification. The safe placement is: business description (once, naturally, e.g., "...operating under NMLS #123456"), your website's footer, and any linked landing page. Consistency matters more than repetition — the number should read identically everywhere it appears, matching your NMLS Consumer Access listing exactly.

Service-area settings: address visibility vs. service radius

Most loan officers should NOT show a physical storefront address on GBP, because most originate from an office that borrowers never visit. Instead, set your profile as a service-area business (SAB): hide the address, and list the specific cities or counties you actually lend in. Two settings decisions matter here: (1) precision — list real cities, not a 50-mile blanket radius, since Google devalues service areas that look implausibly broad for a solo originator; and (2) match your target ZIPs — if you're building exclusive territory in specific ZIP codes (see YPN USA's markets pages for territory examples), your GBP service area should overlap with those ZIPs so the profile and your local landing pages reinforce the same geography to Google.

Photo requirements that actually move the needle

  • Profile photo: a real, current headshot — not a logo. Borrowers click profiles with a human face noticeably more than logo-only profiles.
  • Cover photo: your office exterior/interior or a recognizable local landmark from your service area, not a generic stock rate-chart image.
  • Minimum count: aim for at least 10 photos at launch — headshot, office, team, a few "at work" shots, and any co-marketing events with Realtor partners.
  • Ongoing: add at least one new photo monthly. Static photo counts are a soft signal to Google that the profile has gone dormant.
  • Avoid: rate sheets, APR numbers, or promotional overlays on photos — these can trigger GBP content policy flags in financial services categories.

Hours and attributes: small fields, real impact

Set hours to match when you genuinely respond, not "9-5 on paper." If you take calls until 8pm, say so — GBP's "open now" filter directly affects who calls at that moment. In the attributes section, complete every field relevant to lending: languages spoken, identifies as (if applicable), and online appointment availability if you take virtual applications. Incomplete attribute sections are one of the more overlooked reasons an otherwise strong profile underperforms competitors with the same review count.

Map-pack ranking factors this checklist actually affects

Local Google rankings for lending searches weigh three clusters, roughly in this order: (1) relevance — category accuracy and how well your description and services match the search term; (2) distance — how your set service area lines up with the searcher's location; (3) prominence — review volume/quality, citation consistency, and profile completeness. Every item above touches at least one of those three. Category selection and service-area precision hit relevance and distance directly; photos, hours, and attributes hit prominence by signaling an active, complete, trustworthy business. None of this replaces review generation or posting cadence — those are covered separately in How to Get More Google Reviews as a Loan Officer and 20 Google Business Profile Post Ideas for Loan Officers — this checklist is specifically the setup and configuration layer underneath both.

Setup checklist (do these in order)

  1. Confirm your primary category matches your actual licensing (Mortgage Lender vs. Mortgage Broker).
  2. Add 2-3 accurate secondary categories — no keyword stacking.
  3. Place your NMLS number in the business description only, matching your NMLS Consumer Access listing exactly.
  4. Switch to service-area business mode and hide your address unless borrowers visit in person.
  5. List real cities/counties, ideally overlapping the ZIP territory you're building on your own site.
  6. Upload a real headshot as the profile photo and at least 10 photos total at launch.
  7. Set hours to match actual response availability, not a copy-pasted default.
  8. Complete every relevant attribute field.
  9. Link the profile's website field to a borrower-facing page under your brand, not a generic lender homepage.
  10. Re-check all of the above quarterly — GBP fields drift and get reset more often than most LOs expect.

FAQ: Google Business Profile setup for loan officers

Should I choose "Mortgage Lender" or "Mortgage Broker" as my category?

Choose "Mortgage Lender" if you originate and fund loans directly under your own NMLS record with a single institution. Choose "Mortgage Broker" only if you route applications to multiple wholesale lenders on the borrower's behalf. Picking the wrong one can misrepresent your service to Google and to borrowers comparing profiles in the map pack.

Can I put my NMLS number in my GBP business name?

No. Google's guidelines prohibit credentials, licenses, or keywords in the business name field, and profiles that do this risk suspension. Put your NMLS number in the description instead, worded to match your NMLS Consumer Access listing exactly.

Should I show my office address or hide it as a service-area business?

If borrowers never visit your office in person, hide the address and set the profile as a service-area business with real cities/counties listed. If you do meet clients at a physical location, showing the address can help with proximity-based ranking for nearby searches.

How many photos do I actually need before launch?

Aim for at least 10 at launch — a real headshot, office or team photos, and a couple of local or co-marketing shots — then add roughly one new photo a month. Profiles that stop adding photos after the first week tend to plateau in local rankings faster than ones that keep the profile visibly active.
